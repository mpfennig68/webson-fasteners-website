Include the hole and engagement details

Keep the fit requirement with the pin designation

Specify material, heat treatment or hardness and finish where controlled. If replacing a pin in an existing hole, include the condition of that hole and the approved replacement requirement. A worn sample alone may not establish the original size or fit.

Locating and retaining are separate requirements

Pins can align components, form pivots or retain parts, depending on the design. Identify which function the assembly calls for. For a change in pin style, compare the hole, loads, installation and service requirements before proposing a substitute.

Before you order

Can a spring pin replace a ground dowel?

Do not assume so. The fit, retention and alignment behavior differ. Use the approved pin family or obtain approval for the change.

Can I identify a pin from its loose diameter?

That measurement can help, but the family, tolerance, free state and mating hole also matter. Send the full designation or drawing if available.

What details help with a custom pin quote?

Diameter and length tolerances, end and retention features, material, finish, quantity and current drawing revision.

Send the pin and mating-hole requirements

Include the complete part number or drawing, quantity, delivery date, pin family, diameter and length. Add hole fit, material, finish, installation and documentation requirements.
