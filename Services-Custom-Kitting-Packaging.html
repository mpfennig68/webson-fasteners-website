// BEFORE PARTS REACH THE LINE

Handling that happens before assembly starts.

Hardware usually arrives the way it was purchased, not the way it will be used. Twenty line items land in twenty packages, and somebody has to turn that into what a single assembly needs. That work is real, it is repeated on every run, and it is normally absorbed by the people least able to spare the time.

Kitting moves selected preparation upstream. Components are grouped and packaged according to defined requirements before they ship, so what arrives matches the unit of work it supports. These are the conditions it is generally aimed at:

✓

Components arriving separately

Each line item comes in its own package, and the assortment has to be reconciled against the assembly before anything can be built.

✓

Repetitive counting and sorting

Parts are counted out of bulk containers again for every build, with the same effort repeated each run.

✓

Time spent gathering parts

Staff walk the stockroom collecting items for each assembly instead of working on the assembly itself.

✓

Missing parts found during production

A shortage discovered mid-build stops work at the point where the interruption costs the most.

✓

Excess line-side inventory

Bulk quantities held at the workstation to avoid shortages consume floor space and obscure actual usage.

✓

Multiple orders for related parts

Components used together are ordered, received and reconciled separately even though they are consumed as a set.

✓

Distributing hardware across jobs

Splitting bulk hardware across concurrent jobs or work orders is manual and easy to get wrong.

✓

Packaging that varies by run

Presentation that changes between production runs makes the same task slower to perform and harder to check.

Kitting organizes components according to agreed requirements and shifts selected preparation work away from the production team. It does not eliminate shortages, errors or production delays, and every kit remains dependent on the accuracy of the bill of material it is built from.

// HOW IT WORKS

Five steps from bill of material to delivered kit.

Kitting requirements are reviewed before anything is quoted. What Webson can prepare depends on the products involved, the quantities, the packaging instructions and the documentation the order requires.

  1. 01

    Review the bill of material

    Webson reviews the part numbers, descriptions, drawings, specifications, quantities and documentation requirements that make up the kit.

  2. 02

    Define the kit structure

    The customer identifies how components should be grouped — by assembly, installation, job, work order or production quantity — and which items belong in each kit.

  3. 03

    Confirm packaging requirements

    Webson and the customer review package quantities, labeling instructions, documentation and any special handling requirements before the kit is quoted.

  4. 04

    Source and prepare components

    Approved parts are sourced or released from inventory and prepared according to the agreed kit structure.

  5. 05

    Inspect and deliver

    Kits are checked against the approved requirements and delivered according to the applicable order or program schedule.

// CONFIGURATIONS

Ways a kit can be structured.

The configurations below can be evaluated individually or in combination. Which applies depends on how the customer builds, how material is distributed internally and what the production schedule requires.

Assembly Kits

Every component required for one assembly or sub-assembly grouped as a single unit.

Job or Project Kits

Hardware grouped by job, project or contract so material stays associated with the work it belongs to.

Work-Order Kits

Components packaged against a specific work order and released to match the production schedule.

Installation Hardware Kits

The fastening hardware needed to install a unit, packaged separately from the unit itself.

Maintenance and Repair Kits

Replacement hardware grouped for a defined service, repair or preventive-maintenance task.

Controlled-Quantity Packages

Components supplied in a fixed count per package so consumption is easier to track and issue.

Multi-Component Fastener Kits

Related items such as a screw, washer and nut supplied together rather than as separate line items.

Scheduled or Recurring Releases

Repeating kits prepared to a standing structure and released on agreed dates.

Customer-specific packaging and labeling instructions can be reviewed as part of the quotation. Not every requested format can be accommodated — send the instructions with the requirement so Webson can confirm what is workable before the order is placed.

// QUALITY & PART CONTROL

Control of the parts and of the package.

A kit introduces a second thing to get right. The individual components have to be correct, and so does the completed package. Kits are prepared under the same AS9120B and ISO 9001 certified quality-management system Webson applies to any other order.

✓

Part-Number and Description Review

Items on the bill of material are reviewed and confirmed against the identification supplied by the customer.

✓

Verification Against Kit Requirements

Completed kits are checked against the approved structure and content before release.

✓

Quantity Checks

Counts per component and per kit are verified according to the agreed requirements.

✓

Separation of Similar Components

Items that are visually close — adjacent lengths, similar drive styles — are handled so they stay distinguishable in the kit.

✓

Material and Lot Traceability

Traceability is applied when required by the purchase order or applicable specification. It is not automatic for every commercial component.

✓

Certification and Documentation

Certificates and supporting documents can be supplied according to the component and customer requirements, identified per part number.

✓

Packaging and Labeling Review

Package format and labeling instructions are reviewed against what was agreed before kits ship.

✓

Change Control

When the bill of material is revised, the revision should be submitted so the kit structure is updated rather than assumed.

View Webson’s AS9120B and ISO 9001 certification.

// COMBINED PROGRAMS

Kitting alongside inventory planning.

Kitting answers how components arrive. Inventory planning answers when. Used together on a recurring assembly, they can form a repeatable supply process: the kit structure stays fixed, and replenishment follows the production schedule rather than individual purchase orders.

Vendor-Managed Inventory

Kit components can be held under a managed program so recurring kits draw from agreed stock.

Scheduled Releases

Standing kits released on agreed dates against the customer’s production schedule.

Blanket-Order Programs

A total kit quantity placed under one order and drawn down over the program period.

Local Inventory Support

Agreed components or completed kits held for release from Webson’s Deer Park, New York facility.

Just-in-Time Delivery

Kits timed to production requirements instead of held as line-side stock, where usage supports it.

Bin Stocking

Kitted or loose hardware replenished at designated stocking or point-of-use locations.

These arrangements can be evaluated depending on the products, volumes and program requirements. Read more about vendor-managed inventory and bin stocking.

// FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Kitting and packaging questions.

What is a custom fastener kit?

A custom fastener kit is a group of components packaged together according to a defined requirement rather than supplied as separate line items. The grouping is set by the customer — by assembly, installation, job, work order or production quantity — so the parts needed for that unit of work arrive together.

Can Webson kit components from multiple product categories?

Yes. A kit can be evaluated containing commercial fasteners, electronic hardware, assembly components, Mil-Spec hardware and custom-manufactured parts. Combining categories in one kit is often the reason a program is worthwhile, since it removes the need to gather related items from separate orders.

Can kits be organized by assembly or work order?

Yes. Kits can be structured by assembly, sub-assembly, installation, job, work order or a set production quantity. The customer defines the grouping and Webson reviews whether it can be prepared as specified.

Can Mil-Spec and traceable hardware be included in a kit?

Mil-Spec and specification-controlled hardware can be included. Certification, traceability and documentation requirements should be identified per part number so they are applied when required by the purchase order or applicable specification.

Can Webson source the components as well as package them?

Yes. Webson can quote stocked items, source non-stock requirements and coordinate custom-manufactured components, then prepare the approved parts as kits. A complete bill of material can be submitted rather than each item individually.

Can recurring kits be supplied on a release schedule?

Recurring kits can be evaluated for scheduled releases and combined with vendor-managed inventory, blanket-order or bin-stocking arrangements, depending on the products, quantities and program requirements.

What information is needed for a kitting quote?

Send the bill of material or part list with quantities per kit, the number of kits required, drawings and revision levels, packaging configuration, labeling instructions, material and finish requirements, documentation requirements and the delivery schedule.

Is there a minimum order quantity for custom kits?

There is no published minimum. Feasibility depends on the number of components in the kit, the kit quantity, packaging requirements, production frequency and the overall scope of the program. Send the requirement and Webson will review it.
