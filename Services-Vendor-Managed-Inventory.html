// RECURRING REQUIREMENTS

Inventory support for the parts you buy again and again.

Most production operations buy the same fasteners repeatedly. The part numbers rarely change, the usage is reasonably predictable, and the individual cost is low. What is expensive is the administration around them, and the disruption when one of them runs out. A Webson inventory program can cover frequently purchased commercial fasteners, electronic hardware, assembly components, Mil-Spec hardware and custom parts under a single arrangement.

These are the conditions a program is generally designed to address:

✓

Production interrupted by low-cost parts

A finished assembly held up for a washer, rivet or cable tie costs far more than the component. Managed replenishment is aimed at the items most likely to be overlooked.

✓

Repetitive purchase orders

Recurring items generate a steady stream of requisitions, orders, expedites and invoices for parts whose requirements are already well understood.

✓

Excess safety stock

Uncertainty about lead time and availability tends to be answered with extra inventory, which consumes cash and floor space.

✓

Inconsistent delivery timing

When each order is placed independently, arrival dates drift out of step with the production schedule.

✓

Time spent watching common fasteners

Monitoring bins and shelves for routine hardware occupies buyers, planners and production staff who have higher-value work.

✓

Several suppliers for related categories

Commercial, electronic, assembly, Mil-Spec and custom items are often split across vendors even when one distributor can quote all of them.

✓

Coordinating usage against lead time

Matching consumption rates to replenishment intervals and supplier lead times is difficult to maintain part by part.

✓

Demand that moves

Volume changes, engineering revisions and new bills of material shift requirements faster than static reorder points can follow.

A program is intended to make recurring requirements easier to manage and more predictable. It does not eliminate every shortage, and Webson does not guarantee a specific cost reduction — results depend on the parts, usage accuracy, lead times and how the program is maintained.

// HOW IT WORKS

Four steps from purchase list to managed program.

Webson does not apply a fixed template. Every program is built from the customer’s own purchasing and production requirements, and the scope is agreed before anything is stocked.

  1. 01

    Review requirements

    Webson reviews the part numbers, specifications, usage history, order frequency, lead times and any required documentation. This step establishes what is actually being consumed and how predictable it is.

  2. 02

    Design the program

    Webson and the customer determine which parts are suitable for the program, along with stocking quantities, release schedules and replenishment expectations. Items that are too variable or too infrequent may be better handled as ordinary orders.

  3. 03

    Stock and supply

    Approved products are purchased, stocked or scheduled according to the agreed program, with releases and deliveries following the terms set during design.

  4. 04

    Review and adjust

    Usage and requirements can be reviewed as production volumes, bills of material or delivery needs change, and stocking levels adjusted accordingly.

// PROGRAM OPTIONS

Arrangements that can be combined or used on their own.

The options below can be evaluated individually or together. Which ones apply depends on the products, volumes, facility arrangements and program requirements — not every option is appropriate for every customer or every part number.

Vendor-Managed Inventory

Webson monitors and replenishes agreed items against the program, so recurring parts are handled without a separate purchase order each time.

Bin Stocking

Frequently used hardware is organized and replenished at designated stocking or point-of-use locations, at levels and a review cadence set with the customer.

Just-in-Time Delivery

Deliveries are timed to production requirements rather than held as on-hand stock, where usage is predictable enough to support it.

Scheduled Releases

Quantities are committed once and released against the customer’s production schedule on agreed dates.

Blanket-Order Programs

A total quantity is placed under one order and drawn down over the program period, reducing repeated order placement.

Local Inventory Support

Webson can hold agreed items for release from its Deer Park, New York facility where the program calls for it.

Custom Kitting and Packaging

Components are grouped, identified and packaged by assembly, production stage or bill of material so they arrive ready to use.

✓

Purchase-Order and Specification Review

Program terms, part numbers and applicable specifications are reviewed so releases match what was agreed.

✓

Material and Lot Traceability

✓

Certification and Documentation

Certificates and supporting documents can be supplied according to the component and customer requirements, which should be identified per part number.

✓

Part-Number Control

✓

Receiving Inspection

Incoming product is handled under Webson’s established receiving and quality procedures before it enters program stock.

✓

Documentation Retention

Records are retained according to the applicable quality-system and customer requirements.

// FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Inventory program questions.

What is vendor-managed inventory for fasteners?

Vendor-managed inventory is an arrangement in which Webson takes responsibility for monitoring and replenishing agreed fastener and component items against a program developed with the customer. Rather than issuing a purchase order each time a part runs low, the customer identifies the parts, usage and delivery expectations once, and Webson manages replenishment within those terms.

Which products can be included in a Webson inventory program?

Commercial fasteners, electronic hardware, assembly components, Mil-Spec hardware and custom-manufactured parts can all be evaluated for inclusion. Which specific items are suitable depends on the part numbers, usage, order frequency, lead times and documentation requirements.

Does Webson offer on-site bin stocking?

Bin stocking can be evaluated where appropriate. Frequently used hardware is organized and replenished at designated stocking or point-of-use locations according to the agreed program, levels and review cadence.

Can Webson support scheduled releases or blanket orders?

Yes. Scheduled-release and blanket-order arrangements can be evaluated so that quantities are committed once and released against the customer’s production schedule, depending on the products, volumes and program requirements.

Can Mil-Spec and traceable hardware be included?

Mil-Spec and specification-controlled hardware can be included in a program. Certification, traceability and documentation requirements should be identified for each part number so they are applied when required by the order or applicable specification.

Is there a minimum purchase volume for an inventory program?

There is no single published threshold. Suitability depends on the number of parts, annual usage, order frequency, lead times and the overall requirements of the program. Send the part list and usage information and Webson will review whether a program is appropriate.

Can Webson review an existing fastener purchase list?

Yes. Customers can submit a purchasing list, bill of material or usage history for review. Webson can identify which items are stocked, which require sourcing and which are suitable candidates for a managed program.

How do we request an inventory-program proposal?

Send the part numbers, descriptions, usage information, delivery expectations and any documentation requirements through Webson’s RFQ page. Purchasing lists, bills of material and drawings can be attached to the request.
