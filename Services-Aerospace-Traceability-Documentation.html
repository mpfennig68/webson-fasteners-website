// THE HARDWARE AND THE PAPERWORK

A correct part with the wrong paperwork is not a usable part.

On an aerospace program, hardware that matches the drawing in every dimension can still be rejected at receiving. What makes a component usable is the documented record that establishes its identity, its source and its conformance — and that record has to be built into the order from the start.

The requirements below determine whether hardware arrives ready to accept:

✓

Complete and Accurate Part Numbers

The full part number, including dash numbers and suffixes, since each element carries meaning.

✓

Specification and Drawing Revisions

The revision that governs the order, stated explicitly rather than assumed.

✓

Material and Finish Requirements

Material, grade and finish requirements as called out by the specification.

✓

Manufacturing-Source Information

Source identification where the drawing, specification or customer requires it.

✓

Lot or Batch Identification

Lot identification maintained so material remains traceable to its production.

✓

Certificates of Conformance

Certification identifying the product and its conformance to order requirements.

✓

Special-Process Documentation

Records for plating, heat treatment, baking or coating where those processes are controlled.

✓

Inspection or Testing Documentation

Objective evidence where the order or specification requires it.

✓

Packaging and Labeling

Packaging, marking and preservation requirements that travel with the part.

✓

Traceability Through the Chain

Preservation of the documented link between part and source across each step of distribution.

Documentation requirements should be communicated before quotation and stated clearly on the purchase order. Records that were not requested at the time of order cannot always be recreated afterward — once material has shipped, some information is no longer retrievable.

// WHAT TRACEABILITY MEANS

The documented connection between part and source.

Traceability is not a single document. It is the recorded chain that establishes what a part is and where it came from, assembled from whichever of the elements below the specification and order require.

Manufacturer Identity

Who produced the component.

Manufacturer Part Number

The number under which it was produced.

Customer Part Number

The number under which the customer controls it.

Purchase-Order References

Order references linking the shipment to its authorization.

Lot, Batch or Heat Identification

Identification tying the parts to a production lot or material heat.

Material Information

Material and grade information supporting the specified requirement.

Applicable Specification

The specification and revision the part was supplied against.

Finish or Processing Information

Records of the finishes and processes applied.

Certification Records

The certificates supplied with the shipment.

Quantity and Shipment Information

Quantities and shipment detail completing the record.

Required traceability varies according to the part, specification, customer and program. A commercial fastener does not automatically carry the same documentation as specification-controlled aerospace hardware, and the two should not be assumed equivalent on that basis.

// DOCUMENTATION CATEGORIES

Documents that may accompany an order.

Which of the following applies depends on the component, the specification, the manufacturing source and the accepted purchase-order requirements. Each should be identified during quotation rather than assumed.

Certificate of Conformance

A certificate of conformance may identify the supplied product and certify conformance to applicable order requirements. Whether a manufacturer certificate is available depends on the component and source, and should be confirmed when required.

Material Documentation

Material certifications or related records may be required to support the specified material or grade. Availability depends on the component, source and purchase-order requirements.

Manufacturer and Source Traceability

Documentation may identify the manufacturing source and provide a traceable chain through the distribution process when the specification or customer requires it.

Lot or Batch Identification

Lot identification supports separation and traceability for controlled hardware, keeping material identifiable to its production lot. Individual serialization of each fastener is not implied.

Special-Process Certifications

Plating, heat-treatment, baking, coating and similar records may be required when those operations are controlled by the drawing or specification. See plating and secondary operations.

Inspection and Test Documentation

Inspection reports, test reports or other objective evidence may be supplied when required, available and confirmed during quotation. Webson does not represent that all laboratory testing or dimensional inspection is performed internally.

// CERTIFIED DISTRIBUTION

AS9120B is the aerospace quality-management standard written specifically for distributors, which is the relevant distinction: it addresses the risks that arise in handling and passing on hardware rather than in producing it.

// COUNTERFEIT-PART PREVENTION

Zero tolerance, applied as process.

Webson maintains a zero-tolerance approach to counterfeit parts. In practice that means a controlled sourcing and documentation process rather than a single inspection step — counterfeit material is generally identified by gaps in provenance, not by appearance.

Supplier Qualification

Sources are qualified and monitored under the quality system rather than used opportunistically.

Source and Traceability Review

Source and traceability information is reviewed as part of accepting material.

Part and Document Verification

Part identification is verified against the documentation supplied with it.

Lot Control

Lot identity and segregation are maintained through receipt and delivery.

Discrepancy Review

Discrepancies between product, documentation and order are investigated rather than reconciled informally.

Record Preservation

Records are retained according to applicable procedures and customer requirements.

Escalation of Suspect Product

Suspect or nonconforming product is escalated under documented procedures.

No Unsupported Substitutions

Substitutions are not introduced without the required customer or design-authority approval.

No quality system can guarantee that counterfeit material will never enter a supply chain. What controlled sourcing, documentation review and lot control provide is a process designed to detect it and a record that makes investigation possible.

// PART-NUMBER CONTROL

One character changes the part.

AN, MS, NAS and NASM part numbers encode the configuration of the hardware. A different suffix or dash number is not a variant of the same part — it is a different part, and the difference can fall on any of the following:

Dimensions

Diameter, length, grip and head dimensions.

Thread Configuration

Thread form, size, pitch and class.

Material

Base material and alloy.

Strength

Property class and mechanical capability.

Finish

Plating, coating or surface treatment.

Locking Features

Self-locking elements and their type.

Drilling and Safety-Wire Provisions

Drilled heads or shanks for safety-wire installation.

Dash-Number Configuration

Size and length variations defined by the dash number.

Documentation Requirements

The records the specification requires for that configuration.

Application Suitability

Where the part is approved for use.

The complete part number, the current specification and the customer purchase order determine the acceptable component. A commercial part that resembles a Mil-Spec item is not interchangeable with it on that basis. See Mil-Spec Hardware.

// ORDER & DOCUMENT FLOW

Five steps from RFQ to release.

Documentation requirements are settled at step three, before the order is accepted. Special documentation requested after placement may not be obtainable for material already in process.

  1. 01

    RFQ review

    Webson reviews the complete part number, quantity, specification, revision and documentation requirements.

  2. 02

    Source and availability review

    Appropriate sourcing options are evaluated based on the technical and quality requirements.

  3. 03

    Requirement confirmation

    Questions involving traceability, certifications, approved sources, testing or special processing are clarified before the order is accepted.

  4. 04

    Product and document review

    Received hardware and applicable documentation are reviewed against the purchasing requirements.

  5. 05

    Order release

    Parts and required documentation are supplied according to the accepted order requirements.

// FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Traceability and documentation questions.

What does aerospace fastener traceability include?

Depending on the component and order requirements, traceability may cover the manufacturer identity, manufacturer and customer part numbers, purchase-order references, lot or batch identification, material information, the applicable specification, finish or processing information, certification records and shipment quantities. What is required varies according to the part, specification, customer and program.

Can Webson supply certificates with Mil-Spec fasteners?

Applicable certificates of conformance, material documentation and special-process certifications can be supplied according to the part, specification and accepted purchase-order requirements. Identify the documents needed during quotation so they are secured with the order.

Does every fastener order include the same documentation?

No. Documentation varies by component, specification, manufacturing source and purchase-order requirements. A commercial fastener does not automatically carry the same documentation as specification-controlled aerospace hardware, which is why requirements should be stated before the order is placed.

What information should be included with an aerospace RFQ?

Send the complete part number, quantity, requested delivery date, current drawing or specification with its revision, material and finish requirements, approved-source requirements, certificate-of-conformance and material certification requirements, lot traceability requirements, special-process and inspection or test-report requirements, customer quality clauses, packaging or preservation requirements and any export-control or handling instructions that apply.

Can Webson supply AN, MS, NAS and NASM hardware?

Yes. Webson supplies and sources AN, MS, NAS and NASM hardware including bolts, screws, self-locking nuts, washers, pins, rivets, inserts and retaining components. The website represents only a portion of Webson’s sourcing capabilities — submit the complete part number for review.

Can special-process certifications be supplied?

Plating, heat-treatment, baking, coating and other special-process documentation can be supplied when those operations are controlled by the drawing or specification and the requirement is confirmed during quotation. Availability depends on the component and the processing source.

How does Webson address counterfeit-part risk?

Webson maintains a zero-tolerance approach to counterfeit parts, supported by supplier qualification and monitoring, review of source and traceability information, verification of part identification and documentation, lot control, discrepancy review, record retention and escalation of suspect or nonconforming product. No quality system can guarantee that counterfeit material will never enter a supply chain, which is why controlled sourcing and documentation review are applied throughout.

Can recurring aerospace parts be included in an inventory program?

Recurring Mil-Spec and aerospace requirements can be evaluated for blanket orders, scheduled releases, vendor-managed inventory, local inventory support, custom kitting and long-term supply programs. Documentation, lot-control and approved-source requirements must remain clearly defined across the program.

Where can I download Webson’s AS9120B and ISO 9001 certificate?

Webson’s Quality Management Certificate is available on the certifications page and can be downloaded directly at assets/Webson-Quality-Management-Certificate.pdf.
