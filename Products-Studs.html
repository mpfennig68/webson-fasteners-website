Separate overall length from useful engagement

The full length of a stud does not describe how much thread is available at either interface. Check thread runout, the middle section and any installed projection. Use the drawing’s conventions for special ends and mounting features.

The parent material is part of an installed-stud requirement

For a panel-mounted stud, include the panel material and condition, thickness and prepared hole. For a threaded stud, preserve the material grade, finish and installation controls. Different attachment processes are not interchangeable just because the projecting thread matches.

Production mounting and replacement requirements

Studs are used where the design calls for a fixed or defined projecting thread. Include mating nuts, washers and the installation drawing when available. For a damaged assembly, identify whether only the stud or the complete attachment needs replacement.

Before you order

Does a matching outside thread identify a replacement stud?

No. Check the attachment end, installed projection, material and full geometry.

Can both ends use different threads?

Some designs do. Identify each end explicitly rather than relying on a short size description.

What helps identify a panel-mounted stud?

The manufacturer number, panel material and thickness, hole preparation, mounting form and installed thread projection.

Quote both interfaces

Send the part number or drawing revision, quantity, delivery date and details for both ends. Add parent-part and installation requirements, material, finish and required documentation.
