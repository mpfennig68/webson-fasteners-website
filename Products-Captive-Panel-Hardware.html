Captive panel hardware for service covers.

A captive panel screw stays attached to its panel when it is released from the mating thread. That keeps the screw with the cover during servicing. Webson can quote captive screw assemblies and related hardware from a manufacturer part number or a complete installation drawing.

Check the installed and released positions

Look at the assembly with the cover secured and with the screw released. The screw must reach its mating thread when closed and clear the mating part far enough for the cover to open or lift away. Use the manufacturer’s installation drawing to check both positions.

Replacement parts need the complete designation

Send the assembly part number whenever possible. A loose screw number may leave out the ferrule, spring or retainer used on the panel. Include material, finish and any source restrictions. If you are replacing only one component, identify the remaining assembly so the interfaces can be checked.

Hardware that stays with the service panel

Captive hardware is useful on access covers, electronic chassis and equipment panels that are opened repeatedly. Retaining the screw reduces loose hardware during servicing, but the installed assembly still has to provide the specified engagement and retention. A spring-loaded screw is not automatically captive; retention must be part of the design.

Before you order

Will an ordinary machine screw replace a captive panel screw?

A common thread does not make it a complete replacement. Captive hardware also needs the retention geometry, travel and panel attachment defined for the assembly.

Does a captive screw always have a spring?

No. Some captive assemblies use a spring and others do not. Captive describes retention to the panel; spring action describes how the screw moves.

Can a self-clinching assembly go into any sheet metal?

No. The selected product has requirements for panel material, hardness, thickness, hole preparation and installation. Check those requirements against the panel before choosing the part.

What should I send if the panel hardware is damaged?

Send the complete assembly number or drawing, panel details and photos of the screw and retainer. Identify whether the entire assembly or one component needs replacement, plus quantity and requested delivery date.

Quote the complete panel fastening arrangement

Send the full assembly number or drawing revision, quantity and requested delivery date. Include the panel material and thickness, installation method, mating thread, screw reach and released clearance. List any separately required retainers, springs, nuts or receptacles.
