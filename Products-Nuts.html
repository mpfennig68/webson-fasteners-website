Check the nut envelope as well as the thread

Two nuts can share a thread and still differ in height, wrench size, bearing area or load-bearing requirements. Preserve the complete standard or manufacturer number when ordering a replacement.

Specify the complete nut and bolt combination

Keep the required grade, coating and lubrication conditions with the part designation. If the assembly specifies a particular nut with a bolt or stud, quote that combination. A proposed substitute needs a review of the full joint requirement, not just a thread check.

Purchasing from a part number or bill of material

Include nuts, mating screws or studs and washers in one list. For a Mil-Spec requirement, preserve the full dash number and required issue. The reference pages help identify selected dimensions; availability and order documentation are confirmed with the quote.

Before you order

Can I replace a standard nut with a thin nut?

Only if the joint design permits it. Height and geometry can affect engagement and load-bearing requirements.

Are all locknuts the same?

No. Locking mechanisms, materials, dimensions and service limits differ. Use the specified type and complete designation.

What if I know only the mating bolt size?

Send that information, but also identify the required nut style, grade, finish and available envelope. A drawing or existing part number is preferable.

Send the nut requirement with its mating details

Include the full designation or drawing, quantity, delivery date, thread, grade, style and finish. Add any source, locking-feature or documentation requirements.
