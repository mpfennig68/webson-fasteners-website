// WHEN STANDARD DOES NOT FIT

Reasons a catalog part will not do the job.

Most fastening requirements are met from stock. The ones that are not usually fail on a single detail: a length that does not exist, a head that will not clear, a thread class the catalog does not carry. Webson can review both fully custom, made-to-print fasteners and modifications to standard hardware, and will say which of the two is the more practical route.

✓

Nonstandard dimensions

A required length, diameter, grip or head height that falls outside standard catalog offerings.

✓

Application-specific geometry

Features driven by the assembly rather than by a fastener standard.

✓

Special head or drive configurations

Head styles, drive types or security configurations not available as a stock item.

✓

Unusual thread requirements

Nonstandard pitches, thread classes, partial threading or multiple thread forms on one part.

✓

Tight space or assembly restrictions

Clearance, tool access or envelope constraints that rule out standard hardware.

✓

Specific material requirements

A material or grade called out by the drawing that is not stocked in the required configuration.

✓

Controlled finishes and secondary operations

Plating, coating, heat treatment or patching specified to a particular requirement.

✓

Legacy or obsolete part numbers

Components no longer commercially available that must be reproduced from an existing drawing or sample.

✓

Made to an engineering drawing

Parts defined entirely by the customer’s drawing rather than by any commercial standard.

✓

Consolidation of features

Combining what is currently several components into one manufactured part to reduce assembly steps.

Not every drawing or requested configuration can be produced. Geometry, material, tolerances, quantity and tooling all affect feasibility, which is why each requirement is reviewed before Webson commits to a price or a date.

// HOW IT WORKS

From drawing review through delivery.

Feasibility, price and delivery are established during review, not before it. Webson does not quote a custom part or confirm a date until the drawing and its requirements have been examined.

  1. 01

    Requirement review

    Webson reviews the drawing, specifications, revision level, quantity, material, finish, tolerances and documentation requirements.

  2. 02

    Manufacturing evaluation

    The part is evaluated against appropriate manufacturing methods and qualified sourcing options.

  3. 03

    Quotation and clarification

    Webson identifies questions, commercial considerations and any details that must be clarified before production.

  4. 04

    Production coordination

    Approved parts are scheduled with an appropriate manufacturing source and any required secondary operations are coordinated.

  5. 05

    Inspection and documentation

    Parts and documentation are reviewed according to the purchase order, drawing and applicable quality requirements.

  6. 06

    Delivery and recurring support

    Completed parts are delivered according to the order. Repeat requirements can be evaluated for scheduled releases, blanket programs or inventory support.

// MANUFACTURING METHODS

Processes that can be evaluated for the part.

Webson works with qualified manufacturing sources across the methods below. Which one suits a given part depends on its geometry, material, tolerances, volume and tooling requirements, so the method is selected during review rather than assumed from the drawing.

CNC Turning

Rotational parts — pins, studs, bushings, spacers, shafts — where diameters and lengths are the controlling features.

CNC Milling

Parts with flats, slots, pockets or multiple features requiring controlled material removal.

Cold Forming / Heading

Repeatable shapes formed from wire or stock, generally suited to higher recurring volumes.

Thread Rolling

Threads formed rather than cut, often preferred where thread strength and surface finish matter.

Cut Threading

Threads machined to the drawing, useful for nonstandard pitches, short runs and larger diameters.

Drilling and Tapping

Through holes, cross holes, blind holes and internal threads added as specified.

Broaching

Internal or external profiles, keyways and drive configurations called out by the drawing.

Forming and Bending

Sheet, wire and rod components shaped to a required profile.

Modification of Standards

A stock fastener altered — shortened, re-headed, drilled, refinished — where that path is more practical than new manufacture.

Multi-Operation Components

Precision parts requiring several coordinated operations and secondary processes to complete.

Manufacturing is performed by qualified sources within Webson’s supply network. Webson reviews the requirement, selects and coordinates the source, and manages the commercial, quality and delivery requirements from quotation through delivery.

// DRAWINGS & REVISION CONTROL

A quotation is only as good as the drawing behind it.

Most delays on custom parts trace back to missing or conflicting information rather than to production. Complete technical data at the RFQ stage is what makes a quotation reliable and keeps the part from being made to a superseded revision.

Drawing Number

The controlling drawing identified by number so the correct document governs the order.

Revision Level

The current revision, stated explicitly. Superseded revisions are a common source of rework.

Dimensions and Tolerances

Controlling dimensions with their tolerances, including any critical features.

Thread Size and Class

Thread form, size, pitch, class and length of thread engagement.

Material Specification

The exact material and grade, with the applicable specification where one applies.

Heat-Treatment Requirements

Hardness, condition or property class, and the specification that governs it.

Finish Specification

Plating or coating type, thickness and specification, plus any post-plating requirements.

Special Characteristics

Features flagged as key, critical or otherwise requiring particular attention.

Inspection Requirements

What must be inspected, to what level, and what evidence the order requires.

Required Certifications

Certificates of conformance, material certifications or traceability required by the order.

Packaging or Labeling

How parts should be packaged, identified and presented on receipt.

Where requirements are incomplete or conflict with each other, Webson will raise the questions before quoting rather than assume an interpretation. Design intent, tolerances and acceptance criteria remain the customer’s engineering responsibility — Webson does not substitute its judgment for the drawing.

Controls applied to a drawing-based order.

Custom parts are handled under Webson’s AS9120B and ISO 9001 certified quality-management system, which covers how the order is reviewed, how sources are qualified and how documentation is controlled.

Contract and Order Review

Order requirements are reviewed against the drawing and specification before production is released.

Drawing and Revision Control

The identified drawing and revision govern the order, and changes are handled as changes rather than absorbed silently.

Supplier Qualification

Manufacturing and processing sources are selected and maintained under the quality system.

Part-Number Control

Parts are managed by controlled part number so substitutions are not introduced without customer agreement.

Receiving Inspection

Incoming product is handled under established receiving and inspection procedures.

Material and Lot Traceability

Applied when required by the purchase order or applicable specification, rather than automatically.

Certification Review

Required certificates are reviewed against the order before parts are released.

Nonconformance Control

Nonconforming material is handled under documented procedures, with customer notification where required.

Documentation Retention

Records are retained according to applicable quality-system and customer requirements.

First-article inspection, material certification, inspection reports and full traceability are not automatic on every custom part. Identify each documentation requirement during quotation and state it on the purchase order so it is planned into production rather than discovered afterward.

// FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Custom manufacturing questions.

Can Webson supply fasteners made to an engineering drawing?

Yes. Webson reviews customer drawings and coordinates made-to-print fasteners and precision components through qualified manufacturing sources. The drawing, revision and purchase-order requirements control the finished part.

Does Webson manufacture custom fasteners in-house?

Webson is a certified distributor and sourcing partner rather than a captive machine shop. Custom requirements are managed through qualified manufacturing sources, and Webson coordinates the commercial, quality and delivery requirements from quotation through delivery.

Which manufacturing methods can be used for custom fasteners?

Turning, milling, cold forming, thread rolling, cut threading, drilling and tapping, broaching, forming and modification of standard fasteners can all be evaluated. Method selection depends on the geometry, material, tolerances, quantity and tooling requirements.

Can Webson modify an existing standard fastener?

Yes. Modifying a standard fastener — shortening, re-heading, adding a feature, applying a different finish — is often a more practical path than a fully custom part. Webson can review both approaches against the requirement.

What materials and finishes are available?

Carbon and alloy steels, stainless steels, brass, bronze, aluminum, nylon and other engineered materials can be evaluated according to the drawing and application. Finishes such as zinc plating, passivation, conversion coatings, heat treatment and thread-locking patches can be coordinated where applicable. Availability of a specific grade, alloy or finish depends on the part and the manufacturing source.

Can custom parts include material certifications and traceability?

Certification, traceability and inspection documentation can be supplied when required by the purchase order or applicable specification. These requirements are not automatic — they must be identified during quotation and stated on the purchase order so they are carried through production.

Is there a minimum order quantity for custom fasteners?

There is no single published minimum. Quantity requirements depend on the manufacturing method, material, tooling and setup requirements, secondary operations and part complexity. Send the drawing and required quantity and Webson will review what is practical.

What information should be included with a custom-fastener RFQ?

Send the current drawing and revision, part number, required quantity, estimated annual usage, material specification, finish or coating specification, heat-treatment and thread requirements, dimensional tolerances, inspection and certification requirements, required delivery date and any packaging or labeling instructions.

Can repeat custom parts be included in an inventory or scheduled-release program?

Recurring custom parts can be evaluated for blanket orders, scheduled releases, local inventory, vendor-managed inventory and custom kitting, depending on the part, usage and program requirements.
